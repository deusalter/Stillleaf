The rules the native reader would follow

  1. Never behind text. Vines only grow where the reader reports empty space: outside the page, and in the gap between facing columns.
  2. A margin needs at least 7 characters (about 56 pt) to hold a garden. Narrower margins stay empty rather than squeezing in a cramped vine.
  3. Facing pages grow a spine vine. One vine climbs the fold between the pages and is the chapter progress. The column gap is twice the page gutter: 88 pt at Normal margins, 48 pt at Narrow. Both are wide enough.
  4. Growth follows reading, not time. Vines grow in step with chapter progress, never on their own, and a new chapter starts with bare margins. Page turns don't animate the garden; it only grows by the pages you just read.
  5. Scrolling keeps the garden still. Text moves past fixed vines. Fullscreen just means wider margins and a fuller garden.
  6. Implementation: the shell around the reader's iframe already knows the column geometry (pageGutter, columnCount). It draws one canvas behind the page for the margins, plus a thin overlay over the column gap for the spine, at a low frame rate. Reduce Motion shows the grown state with no animation.
