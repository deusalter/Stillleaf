Window hiddenThe margin garden freezes. Pages read meanwhile grow it in one step on return.

What moves, and how often

  • A new shoot every 30 to 60 s. It grows about one cell every 1.5 s behind a soft glowing tip. One to three grow at once.
  • Old growth recycles. Past its cell budget, the oldest branch withers from its tip back to its stem over about 20 s and lets go of a few spores. The garden keeps the same density all day.
  • Wind. Slow bands of wind cross the window about every 10 s. Leaves flutter and lean as one passes. Stems stay put.
  • Petals. A bloom sheds one about every 10 s. It tumbles down and fades before it reaches anything.
  • Fireflies after dark, pollen motes by day. 8 on the dashboard, 3 around the menu panel and 2 in each reader margin, blinking on 3 to 6 s cycles.
  • The reader keeps its rule. Margins still grow only with reading. Wind, petals and fireflies move there, outside the page.

How the app would run it

  • Settled growth is one raster in 256 pt tiles. A tile is redrawn only when a cell in it settles or withers, about once every 1 to 2 s.
  • Growing tips draw on a small live layer the size of their bounds, at 15 fps, in under 1 ms of main-thread time per frame.
  • Wind costs no frames. Two leaf rasters (still and fluttered) cross-fade under a gradient mask that Core Animation moves, like today’s breathing band.
  • Petals and fireflies are a CAEmitterLayer with glyph images, animated by the render server.
  • Target: 1 to 1.5% CPU at idle on Apple silicon (15 fps × 1 ms), plus render-server time, which shows in the energy gauge.
  • Hidden, occluded or minimised: the live layer stops and emitters pause. Growth is a function of the clock and the day seed, so on return the garden catches up in one step.

How it steps down

  • Low Power (Calm): new growth lands once a minute as one cross-fade, with no glowing tips, petals or fireflies. Wind and breathing stay because the render server runs them.
  • Reduce Motion (Still): today’s garden as one image. It changes only when the window reopens. No wind, petals or fireflies.
  • Off: no garden, as today.

Risk: constant motion beside text. In the reader, wind and fireflies stay at least two cells from the page.

What moves, and how often

  • The pointer. Vines within about 7 cells lean away, leaves open and pollen thins, then spring back over 0.6 s. Nothing moves while the pointer rests or is outside the window. Here a ghost cursor stands in until you move yours.
  • Reading. The dashboard and menu panel gardens fill with today’s goal: 35% of the garden at zero pages, all of it when the goal is met, and then every bloom opens. Each page adds a few cells that fade in. In this preview the reader turns a page every 7 s.
  • Page turns. The reader’s margin leaves rustle in a wave that runs out from the page edge, 0.8 s.
  • Time of day. Dawn is peach, midday neutral, golden hour amber, dusk ember and night cool blue. At night blooms close and pollen dims. The colour shifts every 10 minutes with a 30 s cross-fade. Page paper is never tinted.

How the app would run it

  • Idle cost is today’s: one raster with the breathing band on the render server, and no app frames when nobody acts.
  • Pointer: a tracking area reports moves only over the garden. Only the cells near it redraw, into a 120 pt overlay at up to 60 fps, about 0.3 ms per frame. It stops 0.6 s after the last move.
  • Reading growth re-rasterises once per page, with the new cells in a short fade layer.
  • Time of day is a colour filter on the garden layer, set every 10 minutes.
  • Target: 0% at idle, under 3% while the pointer sweeps through vines, and a short spike per page.
  • Hidden: tracking areas are removed, and the tint is recomputed on return.

How it steps down

  • Low Power (Calm): no pointer lean and no rustle. Reading growth and time-of-day colour stay, since they cost nothing between events.
  • Reduce Motion (Still): no lean or rustle. Growth appears as a plain cross-fade, and the tint changes in one step.
  • Increase Contrast: no tint.

Risk: the lean can feel like a toy if it’s too strong. Keep it under half a cell, and only the glyph swap gives the motion away.

What moves, and how often

  • Between events, the garden is as calm as it is today.
  • Every 4 to 10 minutes something passes, chosen by the day’s seed. No network is used: it isn’t real weather, and history stays on this Mac.
  • Gust (most often, 6 s): wind streaks cross the window. Leaves flip and stems lean as it passes, and loose petals ride along.
  • Soft rain (twice a day at most, 25 s): thin drops fall and stop on the top edges of glass panels and the page with a small splash. The leaves glisten for 20 s afterwards.
  • Drifting seeds (evenings, 15 s): dandelion seeds rise slowly out of the vines.
  • In the reader, weather stays in the margins, waits for a page turn to begin, and never starts while you scroll.

How the app would run it

  • Between events nothing is scheduled. The cost is today’s.
  • A gust is a mask band moving over a pre-rendered “gusted” raster, plus an emitter of streak glyphs. Building that raster takes about 5 ms, once per gust.
  • Rain is two emitters for drops and splashes. The splash emitter is a line along the tops of the glass panels. Capped at 300 particles.
  • Glisten is a short opacity animation on a sparkle layer.
  • Target: under 0.5% averaged over an hour. During an event the app thread is nearly idle while the GPU does the work.
  • Hidden: events are skipped, never queued, so you don’t come back to a backlog of weather.

How it steps down

  • Low Power (Calm): gusts only, as the leaf flutter without streaks. No rain or seeds.
  • Reduce Motion (Still): no weather.
  • Off: no weather.

Risk: rain is the busiest thing on this page. It could be its own setting, off by default.

What moves, and how often

  • The garden grows once, then breathes, as it ships today.
  • Numbers count up when a screen appears (0.6 to 0.9 s, tabular digits so nothing shifts) and roll when they change, like pages and minutes while you read.
  • The goal ring fills dot by dot when it appears. Its leading dot breathes on a 4 s cycle, and while you read, a light runs along the filled dots every 8 s.
  • Hover a sidebar item or button and a ripple spreads through the glyphs behind the glass. A click sends a stronger one.
  • Glass panels shift up to 2 pt against the garden as the pointer moves, for a sense of depth.
  • Reader: the page number rolls and the footer vine grows to its new place on each page turn.

How the app would run it

  • Every transform and fade runs in Core Animation on the render server. Counts use SwiftUI numeric content transitions on macOS 14 and later, and a cross-fade on 13.
  • The leading-dot pulse and the light are repeating animations on one layer each, so they need no app frames.
  • Ripples are the only drawing: a 1 s overlay over the touched area, about 0.4 ms per frame.
  • Parallax moves layer positions only, snapped to whole pixels at rest so text stays crisp.
  • Target: about 0.1% at idle.
  • Hidden: repeating animations are removed. Counts don’t replay on return unless the value changed.

How it steps down

  • Low Power (Calm): no pulse, light, ripples or parallax. Counts stay because they’re short.
  • Reduce Motion (Still): values appear at their final state and the ring shows filled. No parallax or ripples.

Risk: counting numbers can feel like a game. Keep counts to first appearance and real changes.

Recommendation

Make Living garden the base. It’s the only one that answers “stuff constantly moving”, and with tiled rasters, a mask band for wind and emitters for particles, it fits the 2–3% budget.

Borrow three pieces from the others: time-of-day colour and the pointer lean from Responsive, and the counts with the breathing ring from Quiet UI. Keep weather to gusts, and make rain an option.

Use one ladder for every direction: Full, Calm (Low Power, render server only), Still (Reduce Motion) and Off. Calm would replace the current spec rule that Low Power forces Still.
